  <header class="selected-project__header">
    <h2>Insurance Risk Model Auditing: Fairness and Interpretability</h2>
    <p class="selected-project__meta"><strong>Team Project</strong><span aria-hidden="true"> · </span><strong>Role:</strong> Team Lead &amp; Technical Lead</p>
  </header>

  <div class="selected-project__grid">
    <div class="selected-project__body">
      <p>This project investigates the predictive performance, interpretability, and fairness of machine-learning models for multi-class life-insurance risk classification. Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost were trained and evaluated under substantial class imbalance. The best-performing model was subsequently examined using SHAP-based feature attribution, subgroup performance analysis, feature-ablation tests, and controlled input perturbations.</p>

      <p>Although XGBoost achieved the strongest overall performance, with an accuracy of 0.571 and a Macro-F1 score of 0.516, its recall varied considerably across risk levels and age groups. These findings highlight potential concerns regarding uneven error distribution, proxy-variable effects, and model robustness in automated underwriting.</p>

      <p class="selected-project__contribution"><strong>Technical contribution.</strong> I formulated the analytical framework, coordinated the project, and implemented the data preprocessing, modeling, experimental, and evaluation pipelines.</p>
    </div>

<article class="selected-project">
  <header class="selected-project__header">
    <h2>Backtesting Trading Strategies on FAANG Stocks</h2>
    <p class="selected-project__meta"><strong>Team Project</strong><span aria-hidden="true"> · </span><strong>Role:</strong> Team Lead &amp; Technical Lead</p>
  </header>

  <div class="selected-project__grid">
    <div class="selected-project__body">
      <p>This project develops and evaluates rule-based trading strategies using historical data from large-cap technology stocks. Trading signals were constructed from the Relative Strength Index, moving-average crossovers, standardized trading volume, volatility measures, candlestick patterns, and cross-asset return information.</p>

      <p>A Python-based backtesting framework was developed to evaluate three strategies against a buy-and-hold benchmark using cumulative return, annualized volatility, Sharpe ratio, win rate, profit-to-loss ratio, and maximum drawdown. The strongest rule-based strategy achieved a cumulative return of 11.0%, a win rate of 68%, and a maximum drawdown of 9.0%. While it reduced volatility and drawdown relative to buy-and-hold, it did not outperform the benchmark in cumulative return or Sharpe ratio.</p>

      <p class="selected-project__contribution"><strong>Technical contribution.</strong> I designed the research framework, coordinated the project, and led the technical implementation of feature engineering, strategy development, backtesting, and performance evaluation.</p>
